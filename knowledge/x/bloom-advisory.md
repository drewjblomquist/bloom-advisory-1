### One-sentence low-pressure retainer pitch

A simple pitch that offers a clear choice — take the blueprint and do it yourself, or hire us to implement it — positioned without pressure. The seller's willingness to let prospects walk reinforces the credibility of the initial assessment.

- Source: [@coreyganim on X](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528)
- Type: business strategy
- Tags: `assessment`, `client-acquisition`, `confidence`, `consulting`, `low-pressure`, `pitch`, `pricing`, `retainer`, `sales`
- Relevance: 0.80; confidence: 0.90

#### Key principles

- A one-sentence retainer pitch that offers prospects a clear choice (DIY the blueprint or pay us to implement it) is effective because it's simple and puts the control with the buyer. _(author claim; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_
- Don't apply unnecessary pressure in sales; showing you're willing to let a prospect walk strengthens the credibility of your assessment. _(author claim; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_
- Framing an offering as either a transferable blueprint or a paid implementation creates a natural upsell while preserving buyer autonomy. _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_

#### Actionable ideas

- Use this one-sentence pitch template in initial proposals and discovery calls: 'You can take this blueprint and implement it yourself, or hire us to do it — which one sounds better?' _(extracted recommendation; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_
- Package assessments as valuable, standalone blueprints you can hand off; offer a separate retainer for implementation (example anchor: $5,000/month). _(extracted recommendation; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_
- Train salespeople to resist hard-closing and to phrase offers as choices, signaling confidence and preserving trust. _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_

#### Implementation ideas

- Add the one-line pitch as the lead sentence in proposals and on a consulting landing page; follow with a short bulleted breakdown of 'what's included in the blueprint' vs 'what's included in the implementation retainer.' _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_
- Create a short sales script and objection-handling cheatsheet that models the low-pressure phrasing and responses to 'why hire you instead of DIY?'. _(extracted recommendation; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_
- A/B test using the one-sentence choice pitch vs. a conventional hard-close pitch on discovery calls and landing pages; measure conversion and net-new client quality. _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2095545108942950528))_

#### Credibility note

Source is a short public social-media post (single tweet). The advice is practical and actionable but anecdotal; the $5,000 figure is presented as an example/anchor rather than an evidence-backed standard. Recommend validating with A/B tests and your market before adopting pricing.

### Anecdote: consultancies' AI roadmaps often end with a recommendations deck + invoice

Twitter thread observation that many consultancies' AI roadmap engagements follow a predictable sequence (executive interviews, survey, data request, follow-up interviews), produce a recommendations deck with ROI estimates built on assumptions, then conclude with a final presentation and invoice.

- Source: [@mardehaym on X](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492)
- Type: interesting idea
- Tags: `ai-roadmap`, `assumptions`, `consulting`, `deliverables`, `recommendations-deck`, `roi`, `vendor-process`
- Relevance: 0.65; confidence: 0.60

#### Key principles

- Many AI roadmap engagements by consultancies follow a predictable sequence: executive/top-level interviews, a survey, a data request, follow-up interviews, a recommendations deck with ROI estimates built on assumptions, and a final presentation (plus invoice). _(author claim; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_
- Recommendations decks commonly contain ROI estimates that rely on explicit or implicit assumptions rather than validated proofs of value. _(author claim; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_
- Consultancy engagements often deliver strategic artifacts (decks) rather than implemented systems; the engagement can end once the deliverable and invoice are issued. _(author claim; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_

#### Actionable ideas

- When hiring consultancies for AI roadmaps, explicitly require validation of ROI assumptions (e.g., baseline metrics, sensitivity analysis) and define what constitutes ‘proof of value’. _(extracted recommendation; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_
- Negotiate engagement structure to include a funded pilot or phased payment tied to measurable outcomes rather than a single recommendations deck milestone. _(extracted recommendation; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_
- Treat the recommendations deck as the start of work — include contractual obligations for handoff artifacts (code, data, benchmarks) and implementation support if desired. _(extracted recommendation; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_

#### Implementation ideas

- Add contract clauses requiring access to the data, reproducible ROI calculations (with shared notebooks or spreadsheets), and agreed success metrics before paying full fees. _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_
- Design a short, funded proof-of-concept (4–8 weeks) to validate the highest-impact recommendation from the deck, with predefined KPIs and go/no-go criteria. _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_
- Require consultancies to produce an implementation plan that maps recommendations to timeline, cost, required data, and measurable outcomes, not just slide-level recommendations. _(synthesis; [source](https://x.com/mardehaym/status/2095963557150224492))_

#### Credibility note

Single short tweet (anecdotal/opinion) from Mark Ajzenstadt. Useful as a heuristic about common consultancy patterns but not a source of empirical evidence. Verify by reviewing specific vendor contracts, case studies, or multiple client experiences before generalizing.
